This a webpage dedicated to my experience working in a successful group while completing the ten projects in CS540: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence.

This course was taught by Professor Young Woo in Summer 2026.

Tips for using AI in this Course 📝

  • Find the best Large Language Model (LLM) that works best for you.

    • I would reccommend using Claude with its pro subscription.

    • If you are using Claude, be aware of usage limits! Be smart with the messages you send!

  • Use AI to help you LEARN the course material, NOT TO JUST GET ANSWERS.
  • If you're struggling with a question on an assignment, try entering that same question in a new prompt (reprompting).
  • If you're not comfortable with Claude, explore other models such as Grok or ChatGPT.

Suggestions for Projects 💻

  • Form a group for the projects at the start of the semester.

    • DO NOT COMPLETE PROJECTS IN THIS COURSE ALONE. Being in a group and discussing strategies significantly boosts your chances of getting high scores on the competitions.

    • Get out there and find people who are consistent and motivated to do well in this course. Do not wait to reach out until the third week of class.

  • Be cautious of spies.
    • Pick and choose groupmates that you trust. Spies can steal strategies from multiple groups so that their score can be improved, and so that they can hurt other group member's chances of getting a high score on projects.
    • I would reccommend sticking with a group of 5-7 people.

Project #1 - Sphere, Cube Tetrahedron🔴🧊🔺

This was one of the most intimidating projects of this course, mostly because you're learning how to train a network for the first time (on your own or using a Large Language Model).

COMMUNICATE WITH YOUR TEAMMATES! This will make this project less stressful!

Suggestions💡

  • Starting out, train each shape on the same number of images.

  • Train on as many images as you can. Don't go crazy, but get a good number of images at each noise level (try to get a total of 50,000 to 200,000 images).

  • Find the noise threshold at which detecting the shapes is nearly impossible. Look for this range at around 70-100 percent noise.

  • This is the first time where you will talk with your team. Discuss a strategy to decide which images you will submit for the competition.
    • Ideas: Inverted Images, large dark/grey/white spots in images, etc.

  • For the competition, if you'd like to bias your model towards the shape for the designated team, that is also an option as well.

Project #2 - Race Car Track🏎️

Suggestions💡

  • Hint: Think of all possible directions the car could move towards once it is stopped.

  • Use the maximum number of sensors that the project allows without deducting points during the competition.

  • Training your racecar network should be a steady and sequential process:
    • First, make sure your car can race around the track without bumping into the walls. You can also decide what line or path it will follow around the track (close or far from the walls).
    • Next, use the competition simulator to insert practice cars to race against. The purpose of doing this is to train your network to recognize other cars.
    • Keep training your network until you're satisfied with your performance.

  • Talk with your teammates to discuss your strategy for the competition. Remember that you can't hit your teammates cars, and that you will be racing with two cars!

Project #3 - Cancer Patient Tree 🪾

Suggestions💡

  • Use the source listed in the assignment to determine the number of patients and their conditions. This will help you construct the tree covering as much cases as possible.

  • Pay attention to the scoring formula! Your success depends on working with your team members.
    • The more unique test items your team submits, the smaller the penalty for getting a diagnosis wrong.
    • Decide what size each team member's tree should be. Each tree should be similar in size.

  • For the competition, pick out unique cases that other teams are likely to classify wrong. Make sure you and your team can identify these scenarios correctly.

Project #4 - Alien Language Decoding 👽

Suggestions💡

  • Learn the process behind creating your convolutional neural network as described in the assignment.

  • For the competition, talk with your teammates to determine the number of curves in the alien language that you will identify.
    • The process for creating your competition submissions should be similar to A1. Train your network on thousands of random curve images for each digit. Train until you are satisfied with your digit probabilities.
    • After training your images, exchange your results with your teammates and train on their best images.

Project #5 - Sentence Debate 🗣️

Suggestions💡

  • Talk with your team and decide what target sentence that you will support in the debate.

  • Once you have decided your sentence, use the Universal Sentence Encoder. This should give you the 512 dimensional vectors for the assignment.

  • Come up with 10 sentences that support the target sentence, and test their quality using the agent or a large language model.

Project #6 - Emoji Eigenface Suspects 😳

Suggestions💡

  • Your k-value for this assignment is crucial for how well you will perform during the competition. The goal is to identify all suspects while making the k-value as small as possible.

  • Talk with your teammates to decide what group you will identify for the competition. By identifying suspects in the same group, your team can discuss which suspects are the best to go for in your final submission.

  • Once you have your list of suspects, make sure you can identify them across a variety of noise levels.

Project #7 - Drive and Shop Until You Drop 🛍️

Suggestions💡

  • Your decision for what map you will shop for items in is crucial for the competition. Try and use a map that you think other students will not go for, so that you can get as most of the items on your list as you can.

  • Collect all of the items on your list at the cheapest prices and keep each car under its distance limit.

  • Use the competition simulator to get a score estimate of your current path on the map, and adjust until you're satisfied with your score.

Project #8 - Connect 8 Heuristic 🔴🔵

Suggestions💡

  • Test your heuristic answers and networks on the game simulator to see how your network does against the simulator. Test it and verify with LLM until you are satisfied.

  • Notice what depth means while you are answering your questions. A depth of X means to look X moves ahead with the heuristic.

  • Don't be concerned if the autograder disagrees with some of these questions. Try reprompting for each question (this is what I had to do!).

Project #9 - Flappy Birds 🐧🧱

Suggestions💡

  • Simulate your answers through the test pipes after answering the questions and constructing a network using LLM or your own code. Fly through as many pipes as you can!

  • If LLM can't fix the answer for flapping and not flapping the bird (1's and 0's), instead use the feedback from the grader to change your answers. This helped me a lot while completing the project.

  • Get the highest fitness score possible. A score of 200 or higher is good. Try and get at least 250 if you want a high competition score!

Project #10 - Soccer Game ⚽🥅

Suggestions💡

  • Test your networks thoroughly against the opponent. This should be similar to the Connect 4 project.

  • If you get a question incorrect, pay attention to the grader's feedback so that you can know what the ball flag should be. That should help you answer most of the questions.

  • Train your network against your teammates so that you can each other's networks stronger for the competition!
